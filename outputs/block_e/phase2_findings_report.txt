6. PORTFOLIO SUMMARY — TOP 5 FINDINGS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  1. DROPOUT RATE
     31.2% of enrolled students withdrew before completing
     their course — representing 10,156 students in this dataset.

  2. STRONGEST DEMOGRAPHIC RISK FACTOR
     Studied credits shows the strongest linear association with
     dropout (|r| = 0.193). Students with No Formal quals prior
     education had a 42.9% dropout rate — the highest of any
     education group.

  3. SOCIOECONOMIC IMPACT
     Students in the most deprived IMD band (0–10%) drop out at
     37.2%, substantially above the 31.2% average,
     highlighting a clear equity gap that institutions should address.

  4. ASSESSMENT PERFORMANCE AS EARLY SIGNAL
     Dropout students score 7.9 points lower on average in
     assessments (p < 0.05). This makes early assessment results
     a strong, actionable predictor for an early warning model.

  5. INTERVENTION WINDOW
     50% of all dropouts withdraw by day 27 of the course.
     A model that scores students before day -2 could identify
     at-risk students while there is still time to intervene.

7. NEXT STEPS — PHASE 3: STATISTICAL ANALYSIS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Phase 3 will formally test the patterns found in this EDA:

  • Chi-square tests  : confirm categorical feature associations
    (e.g. is gender–dropout association statistically significant?)

  • T-tests / ANOVA   : compare numeric features across groups
    (e.g. are studied credits significantly different for dropouts?)

  • Logistic regression : identify the strongest independent predictors
    of dropout after controlling for all other variables

  • Odds ratios        : quantify how much each feature increases
    or decreases the likelihood of dropout
